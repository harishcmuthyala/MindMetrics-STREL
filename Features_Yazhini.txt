STREL – Naturalistic Dataset and Methods for Studying Mental Stress and Relaxation Patterns in Critical Leading Roles

Mental stress in emergency care leaders and special police leaders


(1) Participant:

The STREL study recruited 25 volunteers under an approved ethics protocol (ERCIC 535 24 02 2024), with 24 completing the study.
Participants included:

14 Emergency Care (EC) leaders (Netherlands)

10 Special Police (SP) leaders (Switzerland)

Each participant was assigned a unique ID (e.g., T001).


At the end of each day, the experimenter debriefed
the participants, extracting an account of what transpired
during the day, noting stressful events, happy moments,
and relaxation periods.

(2) Date:

Data was collected across four days per participant:

One Critical Day (CD)

Two Workdays (WD1, WD2)

One Non-Workday (ND)


(3) Time:

Data was summarized into 5-minute intervals instead of second-by-second recordings.

Example:

06:30–06:35

06:35–06:40

Each row in the dataset represents one 5-minute window.

(4) Day:

Day indicates the type of day:

CD = Critical Workday

WD1 / WD2 = Normal Workdays

ND = Non-Workday


(5) subDC (Sub Distress Condition)

subDC categorizes each 5-minute interval into a more detailed condition within the day type.

It helps distinguish contextual differences such as:

HR = 110 bpm during running → physical activity (not mental stress)

HR = 110 bpm while sitting during crisis → mental stress

subDC provides contextual awareness to correctly interpret physiological signals.


(6) Period:

Time of day is divided into:

Morning (MRN)

Afternoon (AFN)

This helps analyze whether stress increases or decreases throughout the day.

(7) Profession:

Two categories:

EC = Emergency Care leaders

SP = Special Police leaders

(8) Age:

Participant’s age in years.

(9) Gender

Participant’s biological sex (M/F).

(10) BMI:

Body Mass Index.
Important because high BMI can influence heart rate patterns and affect stress labeling.
BMI is used as a covariate to control physiological differences.

Individual:

(1) SNS_Stress:

SNS = Sympathetic Nervous System Index.
It is calculated from HRV(Heart Rate Variability) using Kubios software.

Rule:

If SNSindex > 1 → SNS_Stress = 1 (Stress)

If SNSindex ≤ 1 → SNS_Stress = 0 (No Stress)

This converts continuous SNS values into a binary stress label.

Example:
For Participant T001:
SNSindex = 0.4762
Threshold = 1
→ SNS_Stress = 0 (Not Stressed)

(2) SNS_S:

Number of stressed intervals where SNSindex > 1.

Example:
If there are 100 five-minute windows and 35 exceed threshold:
SNS_S = 35

(3) SNS_NS:

Number of non-stressed intervals where SNSindex ≤ 1.

If:
Total windows = 100
SNS_S = 35

Then:
SNS_NS = 65

SNS_S + SNS_NS = Total intervals

(4) SNSindexThreshold:
It converts a continuous SNS value into binary stress.

Threshold = 1


(5), (6), (7):

NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX): Used as a subjective predictor in naturalistic stress studies because it can provide clues about the
source of observed stress (e.g. time pressure) 
From each participant, they received daily scores for the NASA-TLX six subscales(score range [1 − 20]):

• Mental Demand (NASAMD/ N_MD): Perception of daily mental load 
• Physical Demand (NASAPD/ N_PD): Perception of daily physical exertion 
• Temporal Demand (NASATD/ N_TD): Perception of daily time pressure 
• Performance (NASAP ): Perception of success in executing daily tasks 
• Effort (NASAE): Perceived amount of work expended to achieve said daily performance
• Frustration (NASAF ): Perceived level of irritation in performing daily tasks






OVERALL CONTEXT OF THE STUDY: 

The STREL dataset was designed to study mental stress in real-world leadership roles.

- Data collected included:

Heart Rate (HR)

Heart Rate Variability (HRV)

Motion (cadence, speed)

GPS

Sleep data

NASA-TLX workload scores

Big Five personality traits

Biographic data


Devices used:

Polar Vantage V2 smartwatch

Polar H10 chest strap
Recording at high resolution (1000 Hz).


Main goal:
Detect mental stress (distress) and positive stress (eustress) in naturalistic environments while separating:

Physical stress

Mental stress


Stress labeling methods:
They created stress labels using:

NHR (Normalized Heart Rate):
Stress = HR_Normalized > 2 × standard deviation from baseline

SNS Index method (threshold = 1)




EXTRA NOTES/INFO FROM PAPER:

Participants had to complete trait and
state questionnaires that were implemented in QualtricsXM.

Participants also received a Polar Vantage V2 smartwatch +
H10 chest strap combo -  had to wear these devices for
the four days of observation, including in their sleep. 
The devices were recording geographic, motion, physiological,
and sleep information at high resolution (1000 Hz).

Participants had to complete the biographic questionnaire once - information about the participant’s 
occupation, age, sex, family status, and body mass index (BMI).

The family status of the participants, especially whether they had
preteen children or not, it was important to gain insight into
their activities during non-work periods. 

The BMI of the participants was a covariate of interest because a high BMI
can play a confounding role in cardiac recordings (meaning High BMI affects heart rate patterns), which used for stress labeling. 
The research team also had information about the location of the home and workplace
of the participants, which helped validate the activities of
the participants by cross-referencing the reported locations
with the logged coordinates.

Participants had to complete the Big
Five Inventory once - The Big Five Inventory measures personality
along five dimensions:
• Agreeableness (B5A): The level of friendliness of the
participant with the score range [2, 10].
• Conscientiousness (B5C ): The level of organized nature
of the participant with the score range [2, 10].
• Extraversion (B5E): The level of the outgoing nature of
the participant with the score range [2, 10].
• Neuroticism (B5N ): The level of nervousness of the
participant with the score range [2, 10].
• Openness (B5O): The level of curiosity of the
participant with the score range [2, 10].


Geographic, Motion, Physiological, and Sleep Data: 
In terms of geographic information - the Vantage V2 watch recorded the GPS coordinates. 
Hence, the GPS channel provided the location of the participant over time.

In terms of motion information - the device recorded the cadence and speed. 
The combination of geographic and motion information was used to validate the differentiation 
of physical from nonphysical activities on the move.

In terms of physiological information - the Polar H10 chest strap recorded HRV, HR, and daily HR baselines. 
We used HR signals and HR baselines to compute stress labels during sedentary activities. 

In parallel, we fed HRV signals to the Kubios software to calculate the Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS) Index and
the Stress Index (SI) for developing an alternative set of stress labels - and used this alternative set of stress labels as
a benchmark against our HR-baseline-based stress labeling method. 

Sleep times were determined using physiological analytics provided by 
the Kubios software and participantreported sleep times.
